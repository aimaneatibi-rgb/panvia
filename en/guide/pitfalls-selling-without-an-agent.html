Ten pitfalls when selling without an estate agent — and how to avoid them

Selling yourself works fine; most mistakes aren't a matter of skill but of not knowing where the holes are. These are the ten where it really goes wrong — each with the remedy included.

1 · An asking price from the heart instead of from the land registry

The most expensive mistake, and it happens in the first week. Price on feeling, memory or what the neighbour got and you're almost always too high — and the first two weeks are your shop window. Remedy: check your asking price against real sale prices from the Kadaster (Dutch land registry) before you put anything online.

2 · Dark photos with the laundry still on the sofa

Buyers scroll; the first photo decides. Clutter, evening light and a tilted phone angle cost clicks that never come back. Remedy: tidy up, photograph during the day with the curtains open, from the corner of the room. If in doubt — a property photographer costs € 150 to € 300, not a commission.

3 · Concealing defects

The legally most expensive pitfall. As a seller you have a duty of disclosure: known defects — leaks, subsidence, asbestos — must be shared. Concealing them comes back to you via the civil-law notary or the courts, with damages on top. Remedy: fill in the seller's questionnaire honestly and simply mention known issues in your listing text. Honesty doesn't filter out buyers; surprises afterwards do.

4 · Negotiating without a floor

If you don't know your bottom price, you'll get talked down in the moment — or stubbornly reject every reasonable offer. Remedy: before the first viewing, set your floor and your ideal completion date, write them down, and negotiate from a place of calm.

5 · Only looking at the amount of an offer

An offer is price, conditions and date. A high offer with a financing condition and a shaky buyer can be worth less than a slightly lower offer without conditions. Remedy: weigh all three dials — how that works is in the bidding guide.

6 · Letting verbal agreements float

"We'll sort it out" is not a sale. When a home is sold to a private buyer, only the written purchase agreement is binding — until it's signed, everything can still tip over. Remedy: once you agree, go straight to the notary to put it in writing. Speed is protection here, for both sides.

7 · Not checking the buyer's financing

Three months on and the buyer can't get the mortgage — back to square one, after you've turned other viewers away. Remedy: ask early and simply straight out how the purchase will be financed, allow four to six weeks for the financing condition, and keep interested party number two warm until the conditions have lapsed.

8 · Planning viewings as separate evenings

Give every viewer their own evening and you'll be at it for weeks and lose momentum. Remedy: bundle viewings back to back on one or two days. That saves your calendar — and viewers who cross paths know there's more interest.

9 · Starting the paperwork only at the agreement

No energy label when advertising (legally required), owners' association (VvE) documents that take weeks to arrive, a lost proof of ownership — delays exactly when things need to move. Remedy: collect everything before you advertise; the complete document list is here.

10 · Disappearing after the first week

Listing online, leaning back, and after three quiet weeks concluding that "it isn't working". Remedy: reply to every message within a day (serious buyers are looking at other houses too), refresh your first photo when the clicks drop off, and dare to revise your price if the market says so. Selling isn't placing a listing; it's an extra task for a few weeks — which is exactly why you keep the difference yourself.
